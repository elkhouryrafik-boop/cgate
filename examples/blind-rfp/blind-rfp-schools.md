## 1. Introduction and Background

The Aldermere Development Foundation ("the Foundation" or "ADF") is an international non-profit development organisation implementing education, livelihoods and civic infrastructure programmes in the Republic of Velmara under a country agreement with the Government of the Republic of Velmara. Under the Education Infrastructure Window of its 2024–2028 country strategy, financed from the Foundation's own programme resources and a grant from the Callemont Trust, the Foundation has approved the Velmara Schools Rehabilitation Project (VSRP).

The VSRP will rehabilitate two public primary schools in Chelwa Province:

- **Bunyika Primary School**, Rutema District — a day school enrolling approximately 412 learners (grades 1–7), constructed in 1978, comprising eleven classrooms in three single-storey blocks, an administration block and a teachers' facility. The February 2026 storms destroyed the roof sheeting over two classroom blocks, and the remaining fabric shows long-term deferred maintenance, corroded roof timber, and cracked render. Water and sanitation provision is limited to six pit latrines shared by all learners and staff.
- **Kalenga Primary School**, Sanzawa District — a day school enrolling approximately 355 learners, constructed in 1984, comprising nine classrooms in two blocks and an administration block. Differential settlement has produced cracking in load-bearing walls of three classrooms, which the district education office has closed as a precaution. The school has no on-site water supply; learners carry water from a borehole approximately 600 metres away.

Both schools will remain in operation throughout the works. Rehabilitation must therefore be phased so that teaching is not interrupted. The Ministry of Basic Education (MoBE) of the Republic of Velmara is the Foundation's implementing partner and will remain the owner of the facilities after handover.

The Foundation will procure the rehabilitation works through a separate open competitive tender. This Request for Proposals ("RFP") invites qualified consulting firms ("bidders"; the successful bidder, the "Consultant") to provide detailed design services for the rehabilitation works and to supervise the subsequent works contract through to completion, defects clearance and handover.

This procurement is conducted in accordance with the Foundation's Procurement and Grants Manual (2024 edition). The issuance of this RFP does not constitute an offer or a commitment by the Foundation to award a contract, and the Foundation reserves the right to amend or cancel the process at any stage.

## 2. Objective of the Assignment

The overall objective of the assignment is to deliver, through competitive works tendering, the timely, safe and cost-effective rehabilitation of Bunyika and Kalenga Primary Schools to a standard that is child-friendly, inclusive, gender-responsive and climate-resilient, and to supervise the works to completion and formal handover to MoBE.

Specific objectives are to:

- carry out condition assessments, surveys and investigations sufficient for detailed design at both sites;
- prepare complete, tender-ready detailed designs, specifications, cost estimates and bills of quantities for the rehabilitation works;
- prepare tender documents for the works and support the Foundation in running the works tender, including bid evaluation;
- supervise the works contractor, certify quality and quantities, and administer interim and final payments;
- implement the environmental and social management measures required for works on live school sites, including child safety and safeguarding; and
- deliver as-built documentation, operation and maintenance manuals, and a handover report.

## 3. Scope of Work and Terms of Reference

### 3.1 Part A — Detailed Design Services

The Consultant shall:

1. Review all available documentation, including the 2025 VSRP feasibility study, MoBE facility records, and any surviving original drawings.
2. Carry out a full condition survey of all buildings at both sites, including measured drawings of existing structures.
3. Undertake topographic surveys of both school compounds, including levels, existing services and boundaries.
4. Carry out geotechnical investigations, including trial pits and laboratory testing of soils at proposed footing locations.
5. Perform structural assessments of all existing blocks, including an assessment of the settlement-affected classrooms at Kalenga, and a repair-versus-replacement analysis with cost implications.
6. Develop design options for each school with indicative costs, and facilitate consultation on the options with the school management committees, the MoBE district offices, and community and parent representatives before the design is fixed.
7. Prepare detailed designs for the approved scope, covering structural works, architectural finishes, water supply (including rainwater harvesting where feasible), sanitation (new latrine blocks with separate provision for girls and boys, including accessible stalls and menstrual hygiene management provisions), electrical works (including internal rewiring and solar where feasible), and external works including safety fencing and controlled site access.
8. Apply inclusive and universal design principles: step-free access to at least one complete learning pathway per school, accessible door widths, and at least one accessible latrine stall and washroom facility per school.
9. Screen the works under the Foundation's Environmental and Social Management Framework, prepare site-specific Environmental and Social Management Plans (ESMPs), and prepare a phasing plan that keeps each school in operation during construction.
10. Prepare a cost estimate and bills of quantities, tested against prevailing market prices, and an implementation schedule.
11. Prepare complete tender documents for the works based on the Foundation's standard bidding documents, and support the Foundation through the works tender, including technical evaluation of bids.
12. Submit all drawings and documents in PDF plus editable native formats (CAD and word-processing files).

All designs shall comply with the national building regulations of the Republic of Velmara and, where these are silent, with recognised international good practice.

### 3.2 Part B — Construction Supervision Services

Following award of the works contract, the Consultant shall:

1. Review and approve, or return for correction, the works contractor's submittals and shop drawings within ten (10) working days of receipt.
2. Convene the pre-construction meeting and satisfy itself that the contractor's site mobilisation, traffic management and safety plans are adequate before authorising commencement on site.
3. Maintain full-time site presence through a clerk of works at each school for the duration of the works.
4. Supervise and inspect the works, witness and require testing of materials and workmanship, verify setting out, attend all critical structural stages, and issue site instructions and non-conformance reports as required.
5. Chair monthly site meetings and issue minutes within five (5) working days.
6. Verify executed quantities and certify the contractor's interim and final payment applications; recommend variations only with the Foundation's prior written approval.
7. Monitor the contractor's compliance with the ESMPs and with environmental, social, health and safety standards, including child safety on live school sites and the functioning of the community grievance mechanism.
8. Maintain professional indemnity insurance with a limit of indemnity of not less than USD 1,500,000 per claim for the duration of the assignment, and submit evidence of such cover together with the advance payment guarantee prior to contract signature.
9. Verify tests on completion, issue the snag list, and certify practical completion of the works.
10. Supervise the six (6) month defects liability period, carry out the final inspection, and certify completion of outstanding works.
11. Compile as-built drawings and records, prepare operation and maintenance manuals, deliver a short familiarisation session on routine maintenance to each school management committee, and submit a final handover report.

## 4. Eligibility and Qualification Criteria

### 4.1 Eligible Bidders

Bids are invited from legally constituted consulting firms, acting individually or as a two-member joint venture. Natural persons are not eligible. Bids from local branches of international firms must be accompanied by evidence of the branch's authority to contract and of the parent firm's undertaking to stand behind the bid.

### 4.2 Experience Requirements

To be eligible, a bidder must demonstrate that, within the last eight (8) years, it has:

- completed at least two (2) contracts for the detailed design and construction supervision of rehabilitation or new construction of schools, health facilities or comparable public buildings, each with a total construction cost of not less than USD 400,000; and
- completed at least one (1) such contract in Southern or Eastern Africa or in a comparable operating environment.

### 4.3 Financial Capacity

A bidder must demonstrate a minimum average annual turnover of USD 750,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) over the last three (3) audited financial years. Financial capacity will be verified against the audited financial statements submitted under Section 5.

The bidder shall propose, as a minimum, the following key personnel. Personnel proposed must be available for the periods indicated in the work plan; substitution of any key person during the assignment requires the Foundation's prior written approval.

## 6. Evaluation Criteria and Methodology

### 6.1 Method

Proposals will be evaluated under a two-envelope procedure. Technical proposals will be opened and evaluated first. Financial proposals will be opened only for bidders whose technical proposals achieve or exceed the minimum qualifying technical score. The Foundation reserves the right to request clarifications that do not materially alter the substance of a proposal.

### 6.2 Technical Criteria

| Criterion | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Relevant experience of the firm (Section 4.2) | 15 |
| Qualifications, experience and availability of key personnel | 25 |
| Soundness of methodology, work plan and site supervision arrangements | 20 |
| Understanding of the assignment, including safeguarding on live school sites and inclusive design | 10 |
| Organisation, logistics and local presence | 5 |
| **Total technical points** | **75** |

### 6.3 Minimum Technical Score

To qualify for the financial stage, a technical proposal must score at least seventy percent (70%) of the available technical points (i.e. 52.5 of 75 points).

### 6.4 Financial Evaluation

Financial proposals will be evaluated as submitted in United States Dollars, inclusive of all applicable taxes, duties and levies. Proposals quoted in another currency, or exclusive of taxes, will be treated as non-responsive. Arithmetic errors will be corrected on the basis of unit rates where possible, and the bidder must accept any such correction; failure to accept will result in rejection. The financial score will be calculated as:

25 × (lowest evaluated financial proposal price / evaluated financial proposal price)

The maximum score for the financial proposal is 25 points.

### 6.5 Award and Reservations

The contract will be awarded to the bidder achieving the highest combined technical and financial score. The Foundation's budget for this assignment is in the range of USD 170,000 to USD 190,000; the Foundation may reject any proposal whose financial proposal substantially exceeds the available budget, notwithstanding its combined score. The Foundation is not bound to accept the lowest-priced or any proposal, may cancel the process at any time prior to award, and may negotiate with the highest-ranked bidder where necessary. Unsuccessful bidders may request a written debrief within thirty (30) days of notification of the award decision.
